# Multi-agent harnesses: results report (Phases 5-10)

Generated by `nbs/demo_multiagent.ipynb` from the saved results (no rerun). Specs: `specs/multiagent/`.

## Question and answer

> At a fixed total compute budget, how does team performance change with team size under different communication harnesses, and how robust is each harness when one agent lies?

**Answer (numpy agents, Phases 8-9; confirmatory).** With a shared map (blackboard), output per agent holds up as the team grows at fixed compute (0.266 [0.260, 0.272] welfare per agent-second at N = 64, vs 0.068 [0.067, 0.069] without a channel); teams reach the ideal free distribution faster and agree more; and the same shared map makes them more vulnerable to a liar than peer messages are. All four pre-registered tests were supported. **With language-model agents (Phase 10; exploratory)** the picture splits by task: on a task board where the risk is duplicate work, a shared claim board keeps welfare per agent-turn flat from 1 to 8 agents while teams without one lose two-thirds of it, and the numpy model predicts the ranking of harnesses (rho 0.75); in text foraging no harness helps reliably and the numpy model does not predict the LLM teams (rho 0.02).

## 2. Scaling per harness (Phase 8)

![scaling](report/scaling.png)

At fixed compute (left) every team gets the same total agent-time, so bigger teams run for less time each. Without a channel, or with messages, output per agent collapses as the team grows; the blackboard holds up; the lead starts worst and improves with size. At fixed world time (right) the blackboard gains from size up to 16 agents.

## 3. Robustness to liars (Phase 9)

![robustness](report/robustness.png)

Loss of honest agents' welfare as the share of liars grows. On the blackboard and with the lead, liars do real damage (a quarter of the team lying costs 50% on the blackboard at N = 64); through messages and with no channel, nothing measurable. Private trust weighting was the only defence that paid; shared reputation and sanctions cost more in monitoring than they saved.

## 4. Language-model agents (Phase 10, exploratory)

![llm](report/llm.png)

Claude Haiku 5.5 teams of 1-8, 50 turns per agent, 5 seeds; 47988 API calls, $40.60 in all (cap $150); analysis plan hash `83df7591d1de`.

- **Task board:** without a channel, welfare per agent-turn falls from 0.99 [0.84, 1.07] (alone) to 0.35 [0.30, 0.38] at N = 8 (duplicate work); the claim blackboard's benefit grows with size (+0.23, +0.46, +0.60 at N = 2, 4, 8).
- **Text foraging:** no harness helps reliably; at N = 8 the blackboard costs -0.34 [-0.48, -0.13], with less exploration and lower belief diversity: the herding side of H2.
- **Numpy predicts LLM?** Spearman rho over the 16 (harness, size) cells: board 0.75 [0.55, 0.93], foraging 0.02 [-0.47, 0.45].
- **Stronger model (Claude Sonnet 5.5, board, N 1 and 4):** harness benefit minus Haiku's: blackboard -0.03 [-0.08, 0.01], messages -0.09 [-0.17, 0.04], lead -0.12 [-0.30, 0.01]: smaller at N = 4 but unclear overall, and confounded by Sonnet's many more wrong answers at low effort.
- **Tokens as compute:** at an equal budget of 200 agent-turns, two agents did best; splitting further did not pay. Prompt-only changes (team or credit framing, persistent notes) did nothing clear; notes-only memory hurt.

## 7. Phase 11: the clue board (exploratory)

Each subtask's answer is fixed by two modular clues ("x mod 7 = 3") dealt to different agents, so nobody can solve it alone and sharing (which costs a turn) is load-bearing. Claude Haiku 5.5, 4 channels x N 2, 4, 8, seeds 900-904; plan frozen before the runs (`CLUE_PLAN`).

- **Sharing is load-bearing for LLM teams too:** the blackboard adds 0.39 [0.37, 0.40] welfare per agent-turn over no channel at N = 4 (blackboard 0.55, none 0.16), and keeps it to N = 8 (0.37 [0.33, 0.41]).
- **Messages decay with team size**, as for rule agents: benefit 0.40, 0.31, 0.19 at N = 2, 4, 8.
- **The coordinator fails with LLM agents** (benefit 0.10 at N = 4), although it was the best channel for rule agents at scale: it routes a clue only to agents who have that subtask open when it is shared, and Haiku ignored its suggestion in 62% of its opens, so few shared clues reached anyone (routed clues showed in 74 of 800 observations at N = 4).
- **Numpy predicts LLM** well here: Spearman rho 0.79 [0.74, 0.81] over the 12 (channel, N) cells; the coordinator cells are the main misses.
- **Liar:** one false-clue liar among 4 costs the honest agents 0.36 [0.23, 0.52] on the blackboard and 0.26 [0.21, 0.29] with messages; the blackboard-minus-messages sign agrees with H3, but its interval includes zero (3 seeds).
- **Framing** (team vs own reward wording) changed nothing clear.

Details: `specs/multiagent/phase11/README.md`, `results/multiagent/clue/full/report.md`.

## 8. Phase 12, Track A: better instructions for Claude (exploratory)

Three changes to the Phase 11 clue board for Claude Haiku 5.5, compared paired on seed with the Phase 11 runs (seeds 900-904; plan frozen before the runs, `TRACKA_PLAN`). Welfare per agent-turn, change from Phase 11:

- **Telling agents to follow the coordinator made the team worse:** -0.13 [-0.16, -0.10] at N = 4, -0.16 [-0.19, -0.13] at N = 8. Haiku then followed it almost always, but opened subtasks it knew nothing about and waited for clues the coordinator does not pass on.
- **Passing on the coordinator's collected clues helped:** 0.12 [0.07, 0.16] at N = 4, 0.07 [0.02, 0.11] at N = 8, though the coordinator still trails the blackboard.
- **Showing who works on what did little:** 0.03 [0.01, 0.05] on the blackboard at N = 4.

A coordinator helps by moving information, not by giving orders. Track B (RL training of an open model on the same board) is section 9. Details: `specs/multiagent/phase12/README.md`, `results/multiagent/coop/full/report.md`.

## 9. Phase 12, Track B: training an open model to cooperate (exploratory)

![rl](report/rl_main.png)

Qwen2.5-3B-Instruct on the same clue board (4 agents, blackboard, 15 boards; plan frozen before the runs, `RL_PLAN` version 2): a warm start imitating the rule agents, then 60 steps of RL on a LoRA adapter under three rewards: individual (own solves), team (the team's score) and credit (the solves that one's moves enabled). One training run per reward. Welfare per agent-turn:

- **Imitation did the teaching:** untrained -0.001, warm start 0.27 [0.22, 0.32] (Haiku minus warm start 0.24 [0.19, 0.29]).
- **RL did not beat the warm start** (change from it): credit 0.02 [-0.03, 0.06], individual -0.17 [-0.27, -0.07], team -0.20 [-0.26, -0.14].
- **Individual reward taught selfishness:** shares per held clue fell from 0.92 to 0.15, and 74% of its solves were guesses (3.8 wrong answers per solve).
- **Team reward was unstable:** with no way to tell whose move helped, training collapsed mid-run and the stopping rule halted it at step 56; the evaluated adapter is that run's last, degenerate one.
- **Credit reward kept cooperation intact:** credit minus individual 0.19 [0.09, 0.27], minus team 0.22 [0.14, 0.29]; with three trained agents and one Haiku it was the best partner (-0.16 [-0.20, -0.12] against an all-Haiku team; warm start -0.22 [-0.27, -0.16]).

Better instructions (Track A) and imitation helped; RL did not improve cooperation, and the wrong reward destroyed it. Credit assignment is what kept RL from harming the team. Details: `specs/multiagent/phase12/README.md`, `results/multiagent/rl/eval/report.md`. **Revised by section 10:** a second training seed of credit reward gained about 0.1 over the warm start where the first gained nothing; with two seeds per design, whether RL beats imitation is not settled.

## 10. Phase 13: from diagnosis to fixes (exploratory)

![fixes](report/fix.png)

Fixes for the failures Anthropic's red team reported, each against a prompt-only control and reported as the share of a gap closed against an explicit ceiling (Claude Haiku 5.5; plan frozen before the runs, `FIX_PLAN`).

- **Communication has a price.** On the clue board, where a message costs a turn, a request board widened the pooling gap (-0.18 [-0.21, -0.14] at N = 4) and an instruction to share first lowered output too (-0.14 [-0.18, -0.09]); a fact table did nothing (-0.01 [-0.06, 0.04]).
- **Public reputation beats private verification.** A harness-computed track record of each sender recovered 0.32 [0.20, 0.44] of a liar's damage at no cost without a liar (0.006 [-0.012, 0.026]); a costly private check caught lies but recovered -0.01 [-0.28, 0.19]; a warning did nothing (0.03 [-0.16, 0.20]).
- **On our vote, aggregation without more disclosure.** In a hidden-profile vote, a table of the posted facts closed 0.75 [0.50, 1.00] of the lone-dissenter gap without raising disclosure; enforcing facts first did nothing (-0.02 [-0.31, 0.18]); asking members to speak up closed 0.39 [0.14, 0.59] of the classic gap.
- **The table did not replicate on HiddenBench** (65 tasks, Li, Naito, Shirado, ICML 2026): gap closed -0.07 [-0.69, 0.30]; the speak-up instruction helped instead, through disclosure (hidden facts posted 0.74 -> 0.88; gap closed 0.41 [0.00, 0.71]), so aggregation without disclosure is not a general result.
- **A mechanism fix kept two trained seeds cooperative; gains over imitation are seed-dependent** (revised by section 13: with five seeds the sharing effect did not hold up). Pooled over boards and two training seeds, credit reward gained 0.068 [0.026, 0.112] over the warm start and credit with per-turn advantages 0.069 [0.018, 0.120]; individual reward with a training-only cost per wrong answer kept agents sharing (the loophole priced) and gained 0.053 [0.000, 0.106]. The same credit configuration gained 0.015 with one seed and 0.120 with another: these intervals resample boards, not training runs, and each design had one seed near +0.1 and one near 0, so RL beating imitation is not established; the loophole fix's effect on sharing held in both seeds.

Details: `specs/multiagent/phase13/README.md`, `results/multiagent/fix/full/report.md`, `results/multiagent/rl/eval_p13/report.md`; preprint draft in `paper/`.

## 11. Phase 14: what makes trust information pay, and do the fixes scale? (exploratory)

- **Publicity and being free each help a trust signal, about equally.** Liar damage recovered (four agents, one liar): public and free track record 0.32 [0.20, 0.44], public check 0.14 [-0.05, 0.30], private record 0.13 [-0.13, 0.29], private check -0.01 [-0.28, 0.19]; publicity effect 0.18 [0.04, 0.36], cost effect 0.16 [0.02, 0.30], difference 0.02 [-0.16, 0.20].
- **Both fixes hold from 8 to 32 agents** (a quarter liars): the request board's gap closed -0.16 [-0.21, -0.12] at N = 8, -0.17 [-0.20, -0.15] at N = 16, -0.16 [-0.18, -0.15] at N = 32; the track record recovered 0.30 [0.19, 0.42] at N = 8, 0.22 [0.10, 0.33] at N = 16, 0.31 [0.26, 0.36] at N = 32. Larger teams pool less (pooled share 0.41, 0.38, 0.34) and lose more to the same share of liars (0.47, 0.58, 0.54); a numpy forecast frozen before the runs predicted the first trend and missed the second.

## 12. Phase 15: why do larger teams lose more to liars? (exploratory, $0)

Phase 14's runs replayed from the response cache with a turn-by-turn tracer (plan `TRACE_PLAN`, hash f5c25637acd0), and the honest loss split into eight exactly additive terms.

- **Not dilution.** False posts are as visible at 8 as at 32 agents (change in Haiku's share of open subtasks showing one: 0.02 [-0.01, 0.06]), and Haiku agents almost never pass a liar's clue on (at most 0.25% of liar posts).
- **The numpy forecast failed on the clean trend.** Rule teams lose subtasks no lie touched by 0.37 per agent-turn at N = 8 and 0.23 at N = 32: they pool so much worse as they grow (clean pooled share 0.51 -> 0.36) that liars have less to take. Haiku teams keep more (0.41 -> 0.34), and their losses on poisoned subtasks grow (0.25 -> 0.31).
- **Larger Haiku teams build on lies more often:** the share of answers resting on a false clue rises from 0.79 to 0.91; such answers are wrong 97% of the time. A liar's first post on a clue slot suppresses the true clue in 89%-94% of cases (rule agents: always).
- **No rule-agent variant reproduces the rising damage** (honest-loss change N 8 -> 32: first -0.16, relay -0.23, repost -0.05; Haiku +0.08). For simulation-first screening: forecast the clean trend and the attack effect separately, and validate the clean trend first.

Details: `specs/multiagent/phase15/README.md`, `results/multiagent/liar/full/report.md`.

## 13. Phase 16: more training seeds for the incentive result (exploratory)

Four runs of individual reward and five of individual reward with wrong answers priced (`indpen`), the training run as the unit (plan `RL16_PLAN`, hash 082b3b7c9a31).

- **No hypothesis survived Holm.** Priced minus unpriced: clues shared per held 0.37 [0.04, 0.67] (exact p 0.056, Holm 0.111); solo share, unpriced minus priced 0.39 [0.07, 0.66] (exact p 0.032, Holm 0.095); welfare 0.13 [0.00, 0.24] (exact p 0.063, Holm 0.111). Priced runs against the warm start: -0.05 [-0.16, 0.05] (exact p 0.781).
- **Seed-dependent in both arms:** individual reward drifted selfish in 2 of 4 runs (solo share 0.74, 0.79, 0.25, 0.40); the priced runs shared 1.00, 0.83, 0.47, 0.75, 0.10 of their held clues, one of them drifting selfish.
- **Post hoc, pricing reliably cut guessing** (49-90 guesses per board in every unpriced run, 0.1-15 in every priced run), but whether that kept agents sharing depended on the seed. The earlier claim that pricing the loophole kept agents sharing is withdrawn; the direction holds on average.

Details: `specs/multiagent/phase16/README.md`, `results/multiagent/rl/eval_p16/report.md`.

## 14. Phase 17: trained 3B teams against team size (descriptive)

Step 0 evaluated every Phase 12-16 adapter at 4-32 agents. Every trained team loses most of its pooling as the team grows: the warm start's pooled share falls from 0.22 at N = 4 to 0.07 at N = 32 (the best adapter at N = 32, indpen_s4, keeps 0.11), while Claude Haiku 5.5 keeps 0.34 of 0.45, so the gap widens with size.
- **Recipe pilot (v3, after two diagnosed failures):** the single-pass recipe trained stably (KL to the warm start about 0.01, training welfare 0.28 over steps 21-30); recipes with four update passes per batch halted again. Chosen: p17a; projected main runs 405 A100 hours (to be confirmed).

## 15. Phase 18: colony rules for Claude teams with faulty members (exploratory)

Each clue held by two agents, a quarter of the agents scripted liars; a board that counts how many agents posted each value (confirmed at two, contested when values disagree), an instruction-only control, and assigned guard roles, against the plain board and the oracle track record; N = 8 and 16, 8 seeds, Claude Haiku 5.5 (`COLONY_PLAN` frozen before the runs; exact paired sign-flip tests, Holm over four).

- **Guards are what works:** colony + guards beats the colony board alone by 0.155 [0.135, 0.183] (exact p < 0.001, Holm < 0.001) and the plain board by 0.26 [0.23, 0.29] at N = 8 and 0.20 [0.15, 0.25] at N = 16 (honest pooled share with liars); it recovers 0.72 of the liars' damage against the oracle track record's 0.34, with no ground truth.
- **The board helps a little and the display adds nothing over the instruction:** colony minus plain 0.075 [0.048, 0.102] (exact p 0.005, Holm 0.013), falling from 0.12 [0.06, 0.17] at N = 8 to 0.03 [-0.03, 0.09] at N = 16; colony minus instruction-only -0.001. Contamination falls (H18-2: 0.130 [0.088, 0.171] (exact p 0.004, Holm 0.013)).
- **Safety costs output on clean boards:** about 0.07 pooled share (H18-3, non-inferiority at 0.05, not supported: -0.026 [-0.038, -0.011] (exact p 0.999, Holm 0.999)). Honest agents on the colony board also reposted lies to confirm them (6-8 false posts per 100 shares).
- **Go/no-go (frozen): stop.** The guard advantage shrank from N = 8 to 16 (0.26 to 0.20, not significantly), so the 32-64 agent stage did not run; the numpy forecast called 3 of 4 signs right and missed the guards.
- **Guards at 32 agents (a declared deviation, its own plan frozen first):** guards beat the plain board by 0.229 [0.182, 0.274] (exact p 0.004) with liars, so the advantage did not shrink with size (slope -0.016 per doubling over 8-32, p 0.26); liars took the plain board from 0.36 to -0.08 and guards kept 0.15. Guards also helped against mistaken teammates (0.058 [0.024, 0.094] (exact p 0.023)) and against colluding liars who defeat a two-sender quorum (0.090 [0.054, 0.118] (exact p 0.008)); the clean-board cost was 0.043.
- **Claude liars and unassigned roles (N = 16):** guards beat the plain board against Claude agents told to deceive 0.063 [0.016, 0.104] (Holm p 0.039), recovering 0.70 of their damage; the Claude liars lied less often than scripted ones (an accepted lie in 0.18 of their turns) with the same damage per lie, and did not avoid guarded clues. Offered the guard role without an assignment, no agent took it (0.009 [-0.023, 0.039] (Holm p 0.293); -0.16 against assigned guards): the division of labour paid only when assigned, which is the question for training.

Details: `specs/multiagent/phase18/README.md`, `results/multiagent/colony/stage1/report.md`.

## Limitations

- The numpy results come from a small, abstract world; the LLM results are exploratory, with 3-5 seeds per cell and one sampled reply per request (reruns are exact only through the response cache).
- The task board turned out easy for Claude Haiku 5.5 (solve rate 0.89 at the hardest usable difficulty), so it tests coordination against duplicate work, not division of hard labour (Phase 11 makes information the bottleneck instead).
- The free-riding index does not flag free-riders as defined (Phase 9).
